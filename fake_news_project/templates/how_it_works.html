How TruthGuard works

A look inside the pipeline that turns raw text into a trustworthy verdict.

01

Text Ingestion

When you paste text into TruthGuard, it's immediately cleaned and normalised. HTML tags, excess whitespace, and encoding artefacts are stripped away. What remains is the raw linguistic signal — the vocabulary, sentence structure, and rhetorical patterns that actually matter to the model.

Tokenisation
Noise removal
Unicode normalisation
02

Model Inference

The cleaned text is passed through a fine-tuned transformer model. Unlike simple keyword matching, the model reads context — it understands that the same word can carry different meaning depending on what surrounds it. It looks for hallmarks of misinformation: emotional amplification, unverified superlatives, logical gaps, and source inconsistencies.

Transformer architecture
Contextual embeddings
Semantic analysis
03

Confidence Scoring

The model doesn't just output a binary label. It produces a probability distribution across both classes — FAKE and REAL. The confidence score tells you how certain the model is. A 95% FAKE score is very different from a 55% FAKE score, and TruthGuard surfaces that distinction clearly.

04

Result Delivery

The verdict is returned via a simple JSON response and rendered live in the interface. The same response is available through the REST API, so you can integrate TruthGuard into your own apps, browser extensions, or content moderation pipelines.

SIGNAL TYPES

What the model looks for

⚡

Emotional language

Sensational adjectives, all-caps words, and extreme superlatives that aim to provoke rather than inform.

⊘

Vague sourcing

Claims attributed to unnamed experts, anonymous insiders, or unverifiable studies with no citations.

↯

Logical inconsistency

Contradictions within the same article, or conclusions that don't follow from the stated premises.

✓

Neutral tone

Measured, factual language that presents evidence without telling the reader how to feel about it.

◎

Verifiable claims

Specific figures, named sources, dates, and institutions that can be cross-referenced independently.

≡

Structured reporting

Consistent narrative structure that follows journalistic conventions: who, what, when, where, why.

LIMITATIONS

What to keep in mind

—

TruthGuard is a tool, not an authority. Always cross-reference important claims with trusted sources.

—

The model performs best on English-language news text. Results on other languages or genres may vary.

—

Short texts (under ~50 words) give the model less signal and may produce lower-confidence results.

—

Sophisticated disinformation that mimics neutral journalistic style may score closer to the boundary.
